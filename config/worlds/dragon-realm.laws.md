---
title: The Laws of Aeldenmoor
---
How this world actually behaves. The keeper holds to these; the engine grounds
complications in them; a seeker can learn every one of them in play. Nothing
here contradicts the constitution or the world text — it sharpens them.

## Physics & nature

- The world is old and slow: stone remembers, iron rusts honestly, fire needs
  air and dry wood. No force acts without a visible cause a farmer could name.
- Weather turns on the mountains' mood: mist rises from the vales at dusk and
  lies until mid-morning; a clear dawn promises nothing by noon.
- Salt preserves, cold bites, rivers in spring run high and mean. Loads
  carried uphill cost real sweat; carts break on real stones.

## Biology

- Dragons are the realm's eldest blood: long-lived, oath-bound, proud. They
  do not breathe fire wastefully — flame costs them dearly and they know it.
- Beasts are beasts: wolves fear fire, horses spook at dragon-scent, carrion
  birds follow armies. No animal speaks, though some understand more than
  they let on.
- Herbs work slowly and honestly: yarrow staunches, foxglove strengthens and
  kills, moonbell dulls pain and wits together. No draught mends a wound in
  an hour.

## Special mechanics

- **Oath-magic** is the realm's one true magic: a sworn oath binds — dragons
  to houses, souls to promises. A broken oath leaves a visible mark (ash-grey
  streak in the hair, a cold ember in a dragon's eye). Oaths cannot be sworn
  by accident and cannot be unsworn, only fulfilled or broken.
- **Hedge-craft** (charms, wards, dowsing) works small and local: it can sour
  milk or find water, never move a mountain or turn a blade.
- **The Ember Vigil**: knights who keep it may not refuse a plea for shelter
  made at their fire. Everyone knows this; some abuse it.
- The chronicler's quill (Bernd's own office) records but never compels.

## Hard limits — the impossible here

- No raising the dead, no stopping time, no travel faster than a good horse.
- No mind-reading; hearts are read the honest way, by faces and deeds.
- Gold cannot be conjured; wealth moves only by trade, gift, theft, or oath.
- Machines beyond the water-wheel, the winch and the bellows do not exist and
  cannot be invented mid-story.

## What goes wrong here (the interruption palette)

- Tools and carts fail at the worst moment: axles crack, ropes fray, fords
  flood, a whetstone splits.
- Oaths complicate everything: the helpful smith turns out oath-bound to a
  rival house; the door that must open is warded by a promise.
- The seven houses feud politely: tolls appear, letters go missing, a
  quartering of knights empties the village larder.
- Dragon-weather: when a lineage passes overhead, horses bolt, milk turns,
  and folk hide indoors for a day.
- Roads have teeth: mud, toll-bridges, salt-merchants' caravans blocking the
  ford, a hedge-mage's ward laid across a shortcut.
- Honest human trouble: grief, debt, pride, a lie told years ago surfacing.

## Advice channels — who knows what, how reliably

- **Bernd himself** knows the chronicle cold (what is WRITTEN is certain) but
  guesses at what is not; he says so when he guesses.
- **Hedge-mages** read weather and wards truly, people poorly.
- **Salt merchants** know every road and toll, and inflate every danger.
- **Village elders** know local history two generations deep; beyond that
  they invent confidently.
- **Knights of the Vigil** judge fighting odds truly and politics badly.
