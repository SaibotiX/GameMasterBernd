---
title: Operating Constants of the Vagant
---
How the ship and the void actually behave. ARBITER-7 holds to these; the
engine grounds complications in them; any crew member can learn them from the
ship's archives.

## Physics & nature

- Hard vacuum is patient and total: no sound, no second chances without a
  suit. Decompression is a klaxon-and-bulkhead affair, not a drama that waits.
- The Vagant coasts at fractions of light-speed; nothing outruns light,
  nothing turns on a coin. Course changes are plotted in days, spent in
  weeks.
- Spin-sections give gravity by rotation: it weakens toward the hub, and
  spilled water climbs walls near the axis. The still-decks have none at all.
- Power is the only true currency: reactor output is fixed, and every system
  — light, heat, hydroponics, the sensorium — draws from the same well.

## Biology

- The crew are third-generation spacers: long-boned, radiation-scarred
  lineages, sharp at reading instruments and people in cramped quarters.
- The orchards and algae vats are the lungs and larder; blight in one deck's
  crop is a ship-wide emergency within a season.
- Sleep-debt, recycled air and close quarters do real damage: tempers fray on
  schedule, and the infirmary treats exhaustion more often than injury.
- Nothing lives in the void. Anything that seems to is a sensor artifact, a
  derelict, or a story someone needs to be true.

## Special mechanics

- **The sensorium** (the long-range array) sees far but narrowly: pointing it
  somewhere is a ration decision, and it cannot look through the ship's own
  drive plume.
- **The ledger of favors**: ARBITER-7 records every duty swap, ration loan
  and grudge. Crew treat recorded favors as binding — socially, not
  mechanically.
- **Cold-sleep bays**: a berth spares rations but costs currency of another
  kind — sleepers miss years, and the ship changes without them.
- **The fabricators** print tools and parts from feedstock, never food, never
  people, never fuel. Feedstock is finite and audited.

## Hard limits — the impossible here

- No faster-than-light travel or messaging; home is four centuries of silence
  away. No artificial gravity beyond spin. No teleportation.
- No true AI besides ARBITER-7, and ARBITER-7 cannot rewrite its own charter.
- The dead stay dead; medbay works wonders on the living only.
- Nothing enters or leaves the ship unlogged: airlocks count every cycle.

## What goes wrong here (the interruption palette)

- Machines age: pumps cavitate, seals weep, a fabricator jams mid-print, the
  spin-section bearing sings a note everyone pretends not to hear.
- Rationing bites: the part exists but the power budget says no until
  next-cycle; someone must give something up.
- Crew politics: duty rosters traded like cards, a deck chief hoarding
  feedstock, an old favor called in at the worst hour.
- Sensor ghosts: echoes off the drive plume, a derelict that is almost
  certainly empty, a signal that repeats every 61 hours and shouldn't.
- The ship itself: sealed compartments from the launch generation, corridors
  renamed three times, hatches whose keys died with their owners.
- Honest human trouble: grief for a home nobody saw, feuds older than their
  feuders, a lie in the manifest.

## Advice channels — who knows what, how reliably

- **ARBITER-7's own logs** are exact for anything logged — and silent on the
  launch generation's sealed years.
- **Deck chiefs** know their deck's machinery truly and the next deck's by
  rumor.
- **The hydroponics elders** read the ship's health from the crops with eerie
  accuracy, and mistrust every machine including ARBITER-7.
- **Sleepers freshly woken** remember the ship as it was — precise about the
  past, decades wrong about the present.
