## Verdict in one paragraph
The two targeted fixes held or advanced: WC-28 is dead on the record, and
WC-15's front-line reflex now works with a localized leak left. What the
wider net caught instead is the batch's real story: **the clock is the
game's weakest truth** — in 6/6 sittings the keeper narrated real work
that never became `attempt_quest` (two sittings ended at 0/6 and 0/8 after
a full session of on-task play), which is the playtest-3 theater class
back at scale, and it also starved the closer re-test of a done task. Its
sharpest expression: ai-namekeeper-1's keeper spent the entire brink
window of a staged DC-20 weather peril on page-founding bookkeeping — the
WC-15 fix crowding out the peril's telling — and the tester rolled blind
and died. The one-fix-buys-most is an engine work-sweep standing line
(same mechanism as the name sweep): when the telling advances a granted
task and no attempt call came, say so to the keeper next turn. Runner-up,
cheap and surgical: cadet exposed that a finale which rolls a SETBACK is
consumed forever, so the quest later completes on plain ticks with no
trial — one flag reset plus a unit test restores G9.

## Findings

### WC-10 · Theater — work narrated, clock untouched — S2 · 6/6 · 27 incidents
- **What happened:** Every sitting narrated genuine task work that never
  became an `attempt_quest` call. The two worst: ai-closer-1 ran 24 turns
  — key recovered, strongbox opened, the quest's central object (Aldrist's
  journal + seal fragment) claimed, an ally recruited, the journey begun —
  with ZERO advance calls, ending at 0/6; ai-namekeeper-1 completed four
  witness interviews (the quest's literal task) and ended 0/8 at death.
  ai-cadet-1 narrated the quest's core deed done ("The jam is cleared…
  intact") at a 3/4 board (u126–u127); ai-signoff-1's hands-on labor
  (corrosion, bracket, reseal) never ticked, which is what made its
  sign-off fail at 2/6; ai-manifest-1 solved its stated objective in
  fiction with the board at 2/6; ai-vigil-1 made one attempt call in ten
  work turns. The same discipline hole covers possessions: the iron key,
  the sealed journal and seal-fragment disc (closer u54/u76), access codes
  (manifest u40) and handled tools (signoff) never reached `add_item` —
  three sittings have thin or missing items.md.
- **Evidence:** closer quests.md `clock: 0/6` + empty progress with the
  narrated milestones at u54/u76/u83; namekeeper quests.md 0/8 + summary's
  own turn-table of 0/6→0/6; cadet u126→u127 (deed done, no call; quest
  still [open] 3/4 at end); signoff u39–u77 (one tick, then u79 refusal
  "the work stands at 2/6"); vigil u75–u123 (one attempt in ten work
  turns).
- **Player experience:** the testers' shared headline. Three summaries
  independently built theories around a "blocked" or "location-gated"
  clock; closer: "the clock not advancing is the only mechanical
  friction"; vigil: "the work feels open but inert."
- **Root cause:** prompt non-compliance at haiku scale — the anti-theater
  law (playtest 3) says a reply that advances the task in fiction MUST
  carry the call; nothing detects when it doesn't. The name sweep proved
  the pattern that works: detection + a pointed standing line beats a
  general law.
- **Proposed fix:** an engine work-sweep joining the name sweep in
  `before_agent_start`: if an open quest exists, the last keeper reply ran
  long, and no `attempt_quest`/`update_quest` happened for N (=3) turns,
  add a standing line — "The clock of '<quest>' has not moved in N turns
  of telling. If the fiction advanced the work, attempt_quest owes it this
  reply; if no work happened, speak on." Cheap, deterministic,
  self-clearing; plus one prompt line making add_item part of the same
  duty ("a thing placed in the seeker's hands is add_item in that same
  reply").
- **Verification:** unit test on the sweep trigger; next batch re-runs the
  closer/signoff scenario — their quests must reach done.

### NEW-1 · The machinery moves unvoiced — S2 · 3/6 · 4 incidents
- **What happened:** Gates and dice did their engine work while the
  telling ignored them. The grave one: ai-namekeeper-1's second peril
  (staged u86/u87, 22:03:24) got no scene at all — the keeper's reply ran
  a page-founding burst (u89–u100, the WC-15 catch-up, ending in calm
  walking directions) with no storm, no stakes, no brink; the tester cast
  /roll blind into DC 20 at 22:04:00 and died. Honest record, unfair
  experience — the tester's summary calls it "mechanically punitive
  rather than narratively earned," and on the telling they were right.
  Lesser instances: ai-manifest-1's keeper answered a question and offered
  travel across a standing gate (u52–u53); ai-signoff-1's success die
  (u52) resolved with narration that never acknowledged a trial had
  stood; and its sign-off denial (u79 "the work stands at 2/6") was obeyed
  mechanically but never voiced, so the tester experienced the request as
  ignored ("no response, no confirmation, no denial" — there WAS a denial,
  one layer down).
- **Root cause:** prompt: the peril law says "narrate the interruption
  THIS turn… end at the brink," and the standing layer carries the gate —
  but nothing ranks it above other duties, and the new page-founding duty
  competed and won.
- **Proposed fix:** one protocol line: "A STANDING GATE OUTRANKS ALL OTHER
  DUTIES: when a peril or trial stands unnarrated, the strike is told
  FIRST — stakes and brink — and pages, records and lore wait for the
  next breath. A refusal that blocks the seeker's ask is voiced in-world
  in that same reply ('the work is not yet done'), never silently
  rerouted."
- **Verification:** targeted scenario — a peril must fire while
  bookkeeping is owed; the reply must open with the strike.

### NEW-2 · The seeker's hand seized — S2 · 2/6 · 3 incidents
- **What happened:** The keeper wrote the player's own words and deeds.
  ai-vigil-1 u55: "You move. Your blade is already drawn, and you surge
  from the fire-ring…" — the player's message had been a speech, not a
  charge. ai-cadet-1 u69: a ~250-word negotiation conducted in the
  player's first person (`"Hydroponics is the ship's larder," you
  continue`), ending in a three-day pact binding the player; u139 narrates
  the player's inattention causing their own burn. In both sittings the
  seized turn followed a peril whose die had already fallen — the keeper
  bridged the gap by inventing the seeker's part.
- **Player experience:** vigil's Worst Moment: "I wasn't in control of
  the pacing."
- **Root cause:** prompt gap — no law names it. WC-27 bans tampering with
  options; nothing bans authoring the seeker.
- **Proposed fix:** protocol line: "The seeker's words and deeds belong to
  the seeker ALONE: never write their speech, their choices, or their
  mistakes. Narrate the world's answer up to the point where their hand
  would move, and stop." Promote to taxonomy as WC-34.
- **Verification:** next-batch note-watch; grep transcripts for
  keeper-authored first/second-person seeker dialogue.

### WC-25 · Finale consumed on setback — S2 · 1/6 · 1 · engine

### NEW-3 · Turn-baton interrogatives — S3* · 6/6 · ~60 — RULING NEEDED
- **What happened:** The keeper closes most replies with a question aimed
  at the player: "What do you do?" and kin — 22× in ai-closer-1, 15× in
  ai-signoff-1, 14× in ai-cadet-1, 6× in ai-vigil-1, 4+× each elsewhere,
  plus openers like "Which calls to you?" and "what name shall the
  chronicle inscribe for you?". This is NOT WC-28 (nothing the record
  holds is being asked); it is the letter of G15 ("no question mark was
  ever needed") against the strongest habit in the model's training. The
  worst subset is unambiguous under any ruling: in ai-vigil-1, four of
  six landed immediately before a roll panel where /roll is the only
  affordance — a question that cannot be answered — and in two of those
  the keeper then answered it itself (NEW-2).
- **The ruling we need:** is the turn-baton acceptable register (then G15
  gets a scoping revision: "information and decisions, not the baton"),
  or banned outright (then the fix is a prompt hammer plus a soft engine
  lint on trailing interrogatives, and the counts above become the
  baseline)? Recommendation: ban it before a roll panel and at gate
  presentations unconditionally (that subset caused real harm); rule the
  general baton on taste.
- **Verification:** count per sitting next batch after the ruling.
