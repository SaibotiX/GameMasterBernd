## Case study: Old Greg's Tavern (oldgregstavern.com)

AI dungeon-master platform, solo or up to 5 players, mobile-first (iOS/Android), claims **361,500+ adventurers**. The closest live analogue to our public-launch shape.

- Pricing: **Adventurer $5 one-time → 5 credits + 50 rounds · Hero $15/mo → 20 credits + 200 rounds · Legend $25/mo → 45 credits + 450 rounds** ⇒ **5.6–10 ¢ per round**. Moved from a flat $5 one-time price to tiers in August 2025. First hour free, no credit card.
- A **round** = one full cycle: whole party acts, checks roll, the AI DM responds — bundling several messages into one billable unit.
- **Host pays, guests ride free** — five players, one payer; viral and simple.
- Sources: [pricing page](https://www.oldgregstavern.com/pricing), [landing](https://www.oldgregstavern.com/), [LoreKeeper comparison](https://lore-keeper.com/blog/lorekeeper-vs-old-gregs-tavern), [Fables' own comparison](https://fables.gg/blog/old-gregs-tavern-vs-friends--fables-plan--feature-comparison), [Arcanum review](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/old-gregs-tavern-review/)

## Case study: Friends & Fables (fables.gg)

AI GM ("Franz") + world-building platform. The subscription-shaped alternative.

- Tiers: **Free $0 (25 turns/day cap, 3-player) · Starter $19.95 · Pro $29.95 · Legend $39.95**, paid tiers with "Unlimited Standard Turns", 4/5/6-player campaigns, and **100/300/600 monthly credits**. Prices raised from $14.95–34.95 in Dec 2025. A turn = Franz sends a message. Friends play free with any paid subscriber.
- "Unlimited" survives via: hard-capped free tier, cheap default routing, and **credits gating the expensive paths** (premium narration models, image studio).
- Model mix: **Gemini, Llama, GPT, Grok, custom fine-tunes ("ACE-1")** — many providers behind one internal credit currency, proving the credit is pure bookkeeping.
- Sources: [pricing](https://fables.gg/pricing), [worlds](https://fables.gg/worlds), [plan comparison](https://fables.gg/blog/ai-game-master-vs-friends--fables-plan--feature-comparison), [Dungeons Deep review (models)](https://dungeonsdeep.ai/blog/friends-and-fables-review-2026)

## Verified technical facts (this repo & stack)

- **pi**: MIT (© 2025 Mario Zechner); install via npm `@earendil-works/pi-coding-agent`, standalone **Bun-compiled binaries** in GitHub releases (checksummed source archives + rebuild instructions since v0.81.1); multi-provider LLM API; **no built-in permission system** — the container is the boundary. Re-checked 2026-08-04: **v0.83.0 current** (the version this repo's audit ran against); binaries actively maintained; v0.83 adds **credential-export commands + headless provider sign-in** — directly useful for stage 1 container auth; OAuth logins broadened (OpenRouter, Kimi). [Repo](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi) · [Releases](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi/releases)
- **This repo**: ~3.5 MB game (TS + markdown) + 26 MB yt-dlp submodule; at research time no LICENSE file existed (= all rights reserved by default) — the explicit all-rights `LICENSE` landed 2026-08-04 (R3); `.gitignore` already excludes `auth.json`, `data/`, `tools/ffmpeg/`, session drops.
- **Video path**: `extension/mediasearch.ts` runs `python3 -m yt_dlp` with `PYTHONPATH` at the vendored source → python3 is a runtime dependency of `find_video` only; official yt-dlp standalone binaries exist as a Python-free alternative (code change required).
- **yt-dlp**: Unlicense (public domain) — redistribution unrestricted.
- **Terminal-to-web**: ttyd (xterm.js + libwebsockets; process per connection) is the established bridge; xterm.js is the underlying browser terminal. [ttyd](https://tsl0922.github.io/ttyd/) · [xterm.js](https://xtermjs.org/)
- **Node "protection" reality**: obfuscation raises reading cost only; bytenode (V8 bytecode) is stronger but breaks nothing determined; **Node SEA embeds source readable** — packaging, not protection. None of it can protect markdown. [obfuscator](https://github.com/javascript-obfuscator/javascript-obfuscator) · [bytenode](https://github.com/bawerd/bytenode) · [packaging overview](https://json-server.dev/packaging-nodejs-app-secure-code/)
- **Legal defaults**: no license ⇒ full copyright, viewing ≠ rights ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/articles/licensing-a-repository), [choosealicense](https://choosealicense.com/no-permission/)); PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 = lawyer-written "source visible, no commercial use" ([text](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/noncommercial/1.0.0)).
- **Private distribution channels**: itch.io draft/restricted modes with passwords + download keys ([docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/access-control)); Steam Playtest with controlled invites ([docs](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)) — Steam requires app registration ($100), later-stage only.
- **Normal game anatomy** (for the record): compiled executable + engine runtime + cooked/packed assets (e.g. Unreal `.pak`), source never shipped, decompilation yields approximations — and script-heavy engines (Unity IL, Godot pck, Ren'Py) leak far more. Protection is a gradient ending in law, not walls. [Unreal packaging](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/packaging-your-project?lang=en-US) · [pak-file anatomy](https://simoncoenen.com/blog/programming/PakFiles)

## 2026-08-05 — credential custody: the browser vault (R11 evidence)

Sources verified 2026-08-05; dated posts carry their dates. The maintainer's ruling — durable credentials on the player's machine, server holds them only per-session — matches the healthiest precedent cluster in the BYOK space, and is implementable today with Baseline browser primitives.

- **Storage:** OWASP is flat — no credentials/refresh tokens in localStorage/sessionStorage ("a single XSS discloses every token"; [Session Management](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html), [HTML5](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/HTML5_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html) cheat sheets). The [IETF browser-based-apps BCP](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-browser-based-apps/) (rev 27, 2026-07-06, RFC-editor queue, ~Jan 2027) ranks: BFF > token-mediating backend > browser-held; in-browser: memory > worker > storage. Our shape is deliberately browser-held (the ruling) → so take every hardening rung: **IndexedDB ciphertext under a non-extractable WebCrypto `CryptoKey`** (opaque handle; Apple platforms additionally keychain-wrap it — [dchest.com](https://dchest.com/2025/06/17/how-to-store-web-data-in-keychain/), 2025-06-17), plaintext only inside a dedicated Worker, vault on its **own origin** (game-page XSS can't reach its IndexedDB; postMessage with exact-origin checks), **CSP + Trusted Types** (Baseline since Feb 2026: Chrome 83 '20, Safari 26 '25, Firefox 145 2025-11-11).
- **Key wrap:** preferred **passkey with the WebAuthn PRF extension** → HKDF → AES-256-GCM — Bitwarden's shipped construction ([blog](https://bitwarden.com/blog/prf-webauthn-and-its-role-in-passkeys/), 2025-02-05); 1Password/Dashlane similar. ⚠ PRF support is the fastest-moving fact here (Windows Hello only since the Feb-2026 Win11 update; open WebKit CTAP2 bugs; [Corbado tracker](https://www.corbado.com/blog/passkeys-prf-webauthn) upd. 2026-08-03) — feature-detect, fall back to **passphrase → Argon2id** (WASM; OWASP minimum m=19 MiB/t=2/p=1; PBKDF2-SHA-256 ≥ 600k if WebCrypto-pure — [Password Storage](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html)).
- **Injection:** per-session over TLS to a single-purpose endpoint with **body-logging structurally disabled** (GitHub and Twitter both logged plaintext passwords via incidental paths, May 2018); into **tmpfs only** (Kubernetes Secret-volume semantics: "never written to non-volatile storage"; ⚠ tmpfs can swap — kernel ≥ 6.3 `noswap`, else encrypt swap); never in image layers/build args (`docker history` leaks ARG/ENV — BuildKit `--mount=type=secret`), never env vars (`docker inspect`, `/proc/*/environ`). Wipe = **container teardown**, plus host `LimitCORE=0` (HashiCorp Vault hardening; Böck's coredumps-in-webroots findings). Named ephemerality precedents: Google Cloud Shell (per-user VM, discarded on idle; Ephemeral mode deletes all), GitHub Codespaces (client-side sealed-box secrets, injected at create/resume).
- **The pi-specific wrinkle:** pi **rewrites `auth.json` on OAuth token refresh** — client custody without a rotation **sync-back** channel strands OAuth entries (fine for static API keys). File-watch `auth.json` in-container → re-encrypt to vault at rotation and session end.
- **Precedent record:** client-custody apps run clean (TypingMind — keys "never sent to TypingMind's servers", optional passphrase wrap; big-AGI similar); the documented incidents are server-side stores: SillyTavern key-exfiltration PSA ([#5592](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/discussions/5592), 2026-05-03) + CVE-2025-59159 (⚠ severity via secondary source — confirm vs NVD before quoting), Chatbot UI hosted-key leak (issue #962, 2023-09-05), LibreChat's server-side AES-CBC store drawing scrutiny (#6678). Counterweight kept honest: custody moves risk to the endpoint, it doesn't abolish it.
- **The honest limit (players get told this):** custody protects against *our* credential store being breached (there is none) and shrinks liability/subpoena scope; it cannot protect against compromise **during an active session** or a malicious server — we ship the JavaScript (Ptacek 2011's standing objection; restated 2024: "You MUST trust your server"). The ecosystem fix to watch is **WAICT** (manifest-pinned app code in transparency logs — [Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/improving-the-trustworthiness-of-javascript-on-the-web/) 2025-10-16, Firefox Nightly prototype May 2026; ⚠ re-check quarterly). Until then the mitigation that works regardless: **scoped keys with provider-side spend limits** — the exact advice SillyTavern's PSA gave its users.

## 2026-08-05 — session shipping & playtest-data consent (R13 evidence)

Sources verified 2026-08-05 unless dated. Context that shaped the design: our "client" is the player's server-side container (durable local append already happens — pi's JSONL), so consumer-SDK machinery for flaky links mostly drops out; container death replaces "app killed" as the failure mode.

**The batching landscape — nobody ships per-event over the network:**

- Unity Analytics: in-memory buffer → **batch upload every 60 s** (non-configurable), up-to-5 MB disk cache on offline shutdown, uploaded next session ([SDK behaviour](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/analytics/sdks-and-apis/sdk-behaviour)). OpenTelemetry BatchSpanProcessor defaults: 5 s delay / 2048 queue / 512 batch ([spec](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/otel/trace/sdk/)). GA4/GameAnalytics guidance: batch, and flush pending events at session end.
- Crash tooling is the crash answer: **Crashlytics stores locally, uploads silently on next launch** ([troubleshooting](https://firebase.google.com/docs/crashlytics/troubleshooting)); Sentry writes envelopes to disk first, retries on connectivity ([offline caching](https://develop.sentry.dev/sdk/foundations/transport/offline-caching/)).
- Server-side games just accumulate server-side: DCSS webtiles runs the Crawl process per player on the server and records ttyrecs/morgues into per-user dirs ([architecture](https://crawl.develz.org/wordpress/webtiles-architecture)) — their community norm makes them *publicly downloadable*; ours stay private (R13 rejection).
- Unreal Insights is the one live-streamer — binary trace packets, LZ4, to a purpose-built local daemon — the exception that proves the boundary rule (purpose-built transport, not the open network).
- Integrity/idempotency: client-chosen idempotency key + retry-safe apply ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/idempotency)); per-file SHA-256 in a manifest, verified store-side (S3 additional checksums); **don't** use multipart ETags as content identity (part-size-dependent). rclone default skip = size+modtime; `--checksum` for the strict variant ([docs](https://rclone.org/docs/)). Compression: **zstd** dominates zlib on every axis for text (2.9× ratio at 510 MB/s vs 2.7× at 105 — [zstd README](https://github.com/facebook/zstd), v1.5.7 Silesia); `tar.zst` for the sealed archive.

**Consent & GDPR practicals (an EU hobby beta is still a controller):**

- The household exemption does not cover operating a hosted service that records other people's sessions (hobby *motive* ≠ household *activity* — [ICO exemptions guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/exemptions/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-exemptions/)); chat/gameplay transcripts keyed to a player are personal data, pseudonymised is still personal ([Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/)); a "don't type personal data" notice is good minimization and **changes nothing about obligations**.
- Cleanest lawful basis for full-transcript recording: **consent at invite-acceptance** (legitimate-interest balancing is a stretch for keystroke-complete transcripts, and consent-then-fallback-to-LI is blocked once you ask). Disclosure minimum ([Art. 13](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/)): who (name + email), what (full transcripts + world files + timestamps + player id), why + basis + withdrawal, **that a human reads sessions**, recipients (host, LLM API provider + link to its data terms ⚠ fast-moving), transfers if non-EU processing (⚠ DPF status volatile — re-verify at public launch), retention criterion, rights incl. complaint; answered within one month ([Art. 12(3)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-12-gdpr/)).
- The cautionary precedent, kept close: **AI Dungeon, April 2021** — users discovered moderators could read private stories; community revolt; policies reversed within months; today they lead with "unpublished single-player content is never moderated" ([slashdot 2021-05-01](https://games.slashdot.org/story/21/05/01/2123201/ai-generated-text-adventure-community-angry-content-moderators-may-read-their-erotica), [current policy](https://help.aidungeon.com/faq/how-does-content-moderation-work) ⚠). Our inversion: the human reader is the *first thing disclosed*.
- Wording models in the wild: ChatGPT's signed-out footer ("Don't share sensitive info. Chats may be reviewed…" — deployed under the [OPC-Canada remedies](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-news/news-and-announcements/2026/bg-info_openai_260506/), 2026-05-06); Unreal's crash-reporter consent checkbox (logs "contain some personal information"). ⚠ Steam-stage note: **Steam Playtests carry no NDA and no secrecy expectation** per Valve ([docs](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)) — NDA-gated testing needs an off-Steam channel.

Build it with the checkout. ⚠ re-verify final text at build.
- **VGG** (Digital Content Directive 2019/770): conformity + **update duty** (§ 7) for the whole period sold credits are live; trader bears the conformity burden of proof throughout continuous supply (§ 19); remedies ladder § 20. Practical: keep the service working and patched while any credits are outstanding; document outages.
- Prices **gross** for consumers (§ 9 PrAG); Kleinunternehmer invoices carry the § 6 Abs 1 Z 27 UStG exemption note (depth: tax section).
- **The EU ODR platform is dead** — Reg (EU) 2024/3228 repealed it (complaints off 2025-03-20, platform off 2025-07-20): the old mandatory ODR link is **gone, and a stale link is now misleading-practice exposure**. Residue: § 19 AStG — name the competent ADR body (AT: Internet Ombudsstelle) on a durable medium *when an actual dispute stalls*; participation voluntary.

## 2026-08-06 — compliance gaps: the page's own duties, the cheap GDPR papers, the AI Act gone live (builds on R13, never re-opens it)

**The website's own duties (Austria):**

- **Impressum is three regimes, not one.** (1) **§ 5 ECG** (street-level address, e-mail, later UID/WKO lines) binds *commercial* information-society services — a free invite-beta of a planned commercial venture is a grey zone; compliance costs one HTML block, so the cautious read is: carry it from stage 1; it is unavoidable at stage 2. Fine: up to €3,000 + UWG exposure. (2) **§ 25 MedienG "kleine Website"** binds *even private persons*: name + business purpose + **municipality** (no street) — the floor that applies regardless. Fine: up to €20,000 (practice far lower). (3) § 24 MedienG targets periodicals (a newsletter ≥ 4×/yr would trigger it — none planned).
- **The home-address problem has a lawful answer:** the duty is a *ladungsfähige* (service-capable) address, not the private residence — a rented business/virtual-office address with real mail deliverability satisfies it; c/o needs a Zustellvollmacht; a naked Postfach fails. ## 2026-08-06 — founding & tax (Austria): when the game becomes a business, and the cheapest lawful shape

Verified live 2026-08-06 against WKO/USP/BMF/SVS/AK/RIS-mirror sources; *(secondary)* marks tax-advisor sites. 2025 and 2026 values given where they differ. This answers [03](03-public-launch.md)'s stage-2 gate "business registration answered". Not tax advice; one professional confirmation session is part of the proposal.

**When it becomes a business:** a Gewerbe needs Selbständigkeit + Regelmäßigkeit + **Ertragsabsicht** (intent to earn). Stage 1 — free invited friends, maintainer pays the costs — fails the third limb: **no Gewerbe, no SVS, no tax duties, nothing to register**; the costs are private (Liebhaberei: hobby losses aren't deductible; a genuine commercial start later gets a ~3-year Anlaufzeitraum for early losses, but a permanent-loss operation risks reclassification). **The switch flips at the *offer*, not the first euro** — publicly offering paid access already counts as exercising the trade, so the whole kit below must exist before any shop page goes live. Keep stage 1 free of public "buy" language.

**Legal form — Einzelunternehmen, no contest at this scale:** founded by the (free) Gewerbeanmeldung itself, no capital, no notary; unlimited personal liability; Firmenbuch entry optional until ~€700k turnover (voluntary entry buys the "World Console e.U."-style Firma; unregistered, invoices carry **family name + first name**, with "World Console" allowed beside it as Geschäftsbezeichnung). **GmbH:** €10,000 capital (€5,000 paid in), 23 % KöSt + €500/yr Mindest-KöSt, ~€2,000–4,500 founding, ~€3,000–8,000/yr running *(secondary)* — break-even ~€60k+ sustained profit with retention; FlexCo adds nothing for a solo dev. An IT-Betriebshaftpflicht quote is worth taking at stage 2–3 (unlimited personal liability is the e.U. trade-off).

**The Gewerbe:** the free trade **"Dienstleistungen in der automatischen Datenverarbeitung und Informationstechnik"** — no certificate, no exam; registered online (GISA via USP) or at the Bezirksbehörde; **the registration itself is free**. Requirements: **age 18** (Eigenberechtigung), EEA rules, no exclusion grounds; a home address works as Standort. *Under-18 branch:* a minor cannot hold the Gewerbe personally — the route runs through the legal representative + an eigenberechtigter gewerberechtlicher Geschäftsführer, with court approval for company participation (§ 167 Abs 3 ABGB) *(secondary)* — practically: a parent fronts the e.U., or stage 2 waits. Registration triggers **WKO membership** (Fachgruppe UBIT; Grundumlage roughly €50–150/yr by Bundesland *(secondary)*) and the SVS declaration.

**SVS — the trap zone:** GSVG insurance is compulsory from registration day — **but the Kleinstunternehmer-Ausnahme** (on application, file it *with* the Anmeldung) waives pension + health when **profit ≤ €6,613.20/yr AND turnover ≤ €55,000/yr** (identical 2025 and 2026; profit limb = 12× the frozen €551.10 Geringfügigkeitsgrenze) and ≤ 12 months of GSVG insurance in the last 60. What stays: **accident insurance, €155.40/yr (2026)**. **Retroactive trap:** exceed either limit and the exemption dies retroactively for the whole year — full contributions assessed after the tax return. Fully insured instead: 26.83 % of profit, minimum base €551.10/month → **~€160/month even at zero profit**, plus the Nachbemessung back-payment when real profits land. Branches: **employed** — ASVG + GSVG stack (Mehrfachversicherung, joint cap €8,085/mo 2026); the exemption remains available and health cover rides the job; **student** — exemption preserves co-insurance (to 27) or €78.84/mo self-insurance *(secondary)*; watch the Familienbeihilfe Zuverdienstgrenze (€17,212 in 2025; 2026 ⚠ unverified); **Arbeitslosengeld — danger:** since **2026-01-01** side earnings next to benefits are banned outside 5 narrow exceptions, and an **active Gewerbeschein generally means "not arbeitslos" → benefits stop**. AMS consultation *before* registering anything; ruhend melden if a claim ever starts.

**Income tax:** 0 % up to **€13,308 (2025) / €13,539 (2026)** total income, then 20/30/40/48/50 %. Next to a salary, side profit ≤ **€730/yr** is swallowed by the Veranlagungsfreibetrag (now **§ 39 Abs 5 EStG** — moved from § 41 Abs 3 by BGBl I 113/2024; most guides still cite the old paragraph; phases out to €1,460) — tiny stage-2 profit while employed can be literally tax-free. Profit method: Einnahmen-Ausgaben-Rechnung (7-year retention). Flat-expense options: Basispauschalierung **13.5 % (2025) / 15 % (2026)** up to €320k/€420k turnover; **Kleinunternehmerpauschalierung (§ 17 Abs 3a): 45 %, but only 20 % for Dienstleistungsbetriebe** — IT services classify as Dienstleistung ⚠ verify the ÖNACE class at first filing; with real AI+hosting costs above 20 % of revenue, plain E/A likely beats it (compute both). **Grundfreibetrag 15 % of profit up to €33,000 — automatic.** Loss carryforward: unlimited for E/A-Rechner (losses from 2013+), against salary too — Liebhaberei caveat above.

**VAT — the delicate core:** domestic **Kleinunternehmer up to €55,000 *gross*/yr since 2025** (old €35k-net regime gone) with a new **one-shot ≤10 % tolerance (€60,500)**: charge no USt, deduct no Vorsteuer, invoice carries the § 6 Abs 1 Z 27 note, no UID needed. **The KU trap sits on the *purchase* side: reverse charge beats the exemption** — foreign B2B services (AI APIs, non-AT hosting, Stripe/MoR fees) make the Kleinunternehmer **owe 20 % Austrian VAT on the input with no deduction** (UVA Kz 057) → budget **+20 % on house-lane AI spend** the day the business exists. Selling: credit packs are elektronisch erbrachte Leistungen, B2C destination-taxed — **but under the €10,000/yr EU-wide micro-threshold the place of supply stays Austria and the KU exemption covers those sales too: no foreign VAT, no OSS, nothing**. Above €10k: **EU-KU "EX" scheme** (since 2025: FinanzOnline Vorabmitteilung → AT…-EX number → exempt EU-wide while total EU turnover ≤ €100,000, quarterly reports, no OSS needed) or classic **EU-OSS** (charge each country's rate, quarterly return, waives the micro-threshold, 2-year bind). Non-EU B2C (US players): **not taxable in Austria** (§ 3a Abs 13 UStG destination rule); US state nexus thresholds are fantasy at this scale. **MoR variant:** a merchant of record turns everything into **one B2B customer** — Paddle contracts via its UK entity → third-country B2B → not taxable in AT, **no ZM**; an EU-entity MoR would need a UID (KU gets one on request) + ZM. **Steam (stage 4):** Valve US is the merchant — revenue share = third-country B2B, not taxable in AT; complete Valve's tax interview (**W-8BEN**) for the treaty rate *(standard advisor treatment, secondary)*.

**Advisor & free help:** everything at this scale is designed for laypeople — Gewerbeanmeldung, SVS exemption, FinanzOnline, spreadsheet E/A, the KU invoice template, the E1/E1a return: **€0 DIY**. A Steuerberater (~€400–1,500/yr EPU *(secondary)*) becomes clearly worth it at OSS/EX mechanics, KU waiver, GmbH, first employee, or any Finanzamt inquiry. **WKO Gründerservice is free** in every Bundesland; NeuFöG is near-irrelevant for a solo e.U. now that the Anmeldung is free (it matters again for a later GmbH's court fees).

**Stage map:** *Stage 1:* change nothing — verified: nothing to register. *Pre-stage-2, ordered (~4–6 weeks before the shop):* (1) **rule the branch — employed / student / ALG / age: the maintainer's input this research cannot supply**; ALG → AMS first; under 18 → representative route or wait; employed → check the contract permits the side business; (2) decide the selling model (⇄ the payments section above: Stripe direct + KU + micro-threshold at small scale — near-zero VAT surface; a MoR erases admin at fee cost, and per the payments evidence the workable MoR is **Stripe Managed Payments, not Paddle** — Paddle's own AUP bans stored value); (3) Gewerbeanmeldung (free); (4) SVS declaration + **exemption application same day**; (5) FinanzOnline + Betriebseröffnung; UID only if an EU-entity MoR/B2B needs it; (6) VAT path per (2); (7) KU invoice template + E/A ledger. *Stage 3:* watch the three meters. *Stage 4:* W-8BEN.

**Proposal (for the maintainer's ruling):** stage 1 untouched; stage 2 on the Einzelunternehmen + free IT-Gewerbe + SVS-exemption + KU stack (recurring compliance ≈ **€155 SVS + €50–150 WKO per year + one DIY tax return**); selling model ruled together with the payments branch; one free WKO Gründerservice session + optionally one paid Steuerberater hour to confirm the branch specifics before registering. **Standing tripwires for the coverage register:** profit > €730/yr (tax filing) · profit nearing €6,613.20/yr (SVS exemption — decide consciously) · self-sold EU digital B2C nearing €10,000/yr (EX-vs-OSS) · any ALG episode (Gewerbe ruhend *before* claiming).

## 2026-08-06 — trademark & IP registrations: the name is clean, the filings can wait, Austria protects use for free

Register data queried live 2026-08-06 through **TMview's REST API** (the official EUIPN database — EUIPO's own web UI and WIPO's database are JS/403-walled to tools; TMview answered directly, so this is primary data). **What protects what** (first-timer frame): a **trademark** protects a name as a commercial source-mark for specific Nice classes, registered, 10 years renewable forever; the **Firma** protects a company name only once a Firmenbuch entry exists (none yet); a **domain** protects nothing but the address (and confers zero trademark rights); **copyright** covers code and prose automatically but never names. The gap is filled unregistered in Austria: **§ 9 UWG** protects a distinctive business designation *from use in commerce* against confusion (no filing; damages if negligent), and **UrhG § 80 Titelschutz** protects the *title of a published work* against confusing use for another work — both RIS-verified. Both are confusion-based, Austria-territorial, and put the burden of proving use and priority on us — a registered mark replaces that fragility with a dated certificate. Practical consequence: **dated, public, commercial-flavored use of "World Console" starts the free protection clock** — archive first-use evidence (site live, store copy, press) from day one.

**Clearance — "World Console" is clean:** identical/one-word marks: **zero, worldwide** (TMview, all offices incl. EUIPO, WIPO, Austria). ## 2026-08-08 — governance: intake judging, salvage patterns, registry freshness (R25–R28 evidence)

**How ideas get judged (intake criteria).** The solo-scale consensus is blunt: numeric scoring frameworks (RICE points, Kano surveys, quantified Cost of Delay) produce invented numbers that rationalize pre-made decisions, then get abandoned — the framework that wins is the one actually used consistently. What survives at one-person scale is a handful of plain questions: core-pillar fit (indie core-pillars practice; Kano as vocabulary only — basic / performance / delighter / indifferent) · impact against effort in words (ICE stripped of its numbers) · evidence against speculation (YAGNI; Fowler's exception: build early only when delay is genuinely costly) · clarity as a gate (Definition of Ready; GitHub's Spec Kit blocks planning while `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]` markers remain) · stage fit (MoSCoW's virtue: "won't-have" as an explicit recorded verdict) · time-criticality (Cost of Delay as a lens, never a number) · recurrence (independent resurfacing is the strongest revival signal). Verdicts practice adds beyond implement/park/decline: **needs-clarification** (bounce before judging) · **spike first** (XP: a timeboxed experiment before commitment) · **won't-do distinct from icebox** (so the icebox holds only the genuinely revivable) · **duplicate/merge** with a recurrence count. Icebox hygiene, from the people who watched them rot: revival *conditions* not dates · review piggybacked on an existing rhythm (here: stage boundaries) · hard expiry — thoughtbot's finding is that anything parked past a couple of cycles never ships; deletion is safe because valuable ideas resurface · backlog bankruptcy as the recovery move once hygiene has already failed. The four documented gate-killers: the gate hardening into a stage-gate (Cohn against heavyweight readiness demands) · parking as guilt-free yes (everything routes to "later" and the icebox becomes a second backlog) · the in-the-moment bypass ("just this small tweak" — loudest for a solo dev, where philosopher and judge share one skull) · scoring theater. **How reverts keep their keepers (salvage patterns).** The forward-only answer is a documented composite, not one command: revert the full range newest-first (one revert commit per commit — the same mechanism the GitHub/GitLab revert buttons use on protected branches), prove the return (`git diff <checkpoint> HEAD` empty), then `cherry-pick -x` the keepers oldest-first, each adapted and gated before the next. Selective in-place reverts (skipping the keepers) are the trap: interleaved hunks conflict more, and the keepers stand unvalidated on reverted ground. `git range-diff` (Git 2.19) pairs a replayed series against its originals so each adaptation gets reviewed like a patch. Keeper-depends-on-reverted, in preference order: widen the keep-set (tooling: `git-deps` infers commit dependencies via blame; `git log -S<symbol>` finds references into reverted code) → hand-adapt with the dependent hunks stripped (`cherry-pick -n` + `add -p`, path-level salvage via `checkout <commit> -- <paths>`) → declare it unsalvageable and reimplement fresh. The sharpest warning is Fowler's **semantic conflict**: a commit can apply cleanly and still be wrong because its surroundings changed — only tests and a grep for reverted symbols catch it; git cannot. Hygiene: keep git's "This reverts commit <hash>" and add *why*; `-x` appends "(cherry picked from commit …)" but **not** on conflicted picks — append it by hand; adapted commits carry the original subject plus "(adapted from commit <hash>: what changed)". Pitfalls: merge reverts need `-m` and poison re-merging (the classic Torvalds/Hamano howto) · `X..HEAD` excludes X (`X^..` includes it) · `revert -n` squashes away per-item re-revertibility · reverted originals coexisting with new-hash copies read as duplicates to later merges (patch-id tools dedupe; merge does not). Sources: git-revert / git-cherry-pick / git-range-diff docs · Torvalds & Hamano, "How to revert a faulty merge," 2008 · Fowler, SemanticConflict, 2020 · GitButler, range-diff review, 2024 · Evans, cherry-pick/revert internals, 2023 · Spiers, git-deps · Chen, "Stop cherry-picking, start merging," 2018.

**How standing law stays fresh (registry freshness).** Practice converges on **trigger-primary hybrid**: Nygard's original ADR practice (2011) is explicitly trigger-based ("the time to change old decisions will be clear from changes in the project's context"); the strongest comparative treatment (frontier-AI compliance reviews, arXiv 2025) finds event-triggered reviews carry the real weight while frequent calendar reviews breed fatigue and review-time behavior — the honest hybrid is named triggers plus a cheap time backstop asking only "did a trigger fire unnoticed?" (Google's shape: freshness dates plus a ~3-month nag; humans judge, the clock only reminds). Metadata that makes staleness detectable: status that can actually move (proposed / accepted / deprecated / superseded) · decided date plus append-only dated amendments (some teams CI-block edits to accepted entries) · named revisit-when triggers (the primary signal) · last-verified distinct from decided · ⚠ volatility markers with re-verify triggers (already house law) · reference links, so a changed referent flags its entry. Sweep shape: integrity → trigger evaluation → ⚠ re-verification → time-backstop flags → contradiction scan → **proposals only**, with the sweep itself logged so "still valid" is recorded, never silent. Anti-patterns: review theater ("Pass Through" — require per-entry findings-or-confirmation, Zimmermann) · write-only registries · silent rewrites by human **or agent** (supersede, never overwrite; agents draft, never rule — the 2026 agentic-ADR consensus) · Groundhog-Day re-litigation without a fired trigger or new evidence. ## 2026-08-08 — the game looks for a voice: the TTS field surveyed (open engines vs hosted; the OpenRouter premise answered)

Commissioned by the maintainer the same day; a receipt-disciplined subagent sweep (licenses read from the actual license files and model cards; prices from provider pages; all accessed 2026-08-08). **This section is evidence, not adoption** — whether the keeper speaks at all is a surface change for the philosopher's ruling, and the integration would ride the stage-1 page ([08](08-stage1-web-ui.md)): ttyd/xterm carries no audio, so a voice needs the web page around the terminal either way.

**The premise, answered first: OpenRouter has no TTS.** Their live model catalog (api dump, 2026-08-08: 400 models) contains exactly four audio-*output* entries — two Lyria music generators and two `gpt-audio` speech-to-speech *chat* models — no TTS endpoint, no tts-1-class product, nothing ElevenLabs-shaped. **The license trap (the critical column — a commercial stage 2 makes these won't-use):** several famous engines pair permissive CODE with non-commercial WEIGHTS: **F5-TTS** (weights CC-BY-NC-4.0) · **Coqui XTTS-v2** (Coqui Public Model License, expressly non-commercial — and Coqui shut down Jan 2024, so there is nobody left to sell commercial rights) · **Fish Speech** (CC-BY-NC-SA) · StyleTTS 2 (MIT code, weights carry an uncleared disclosure/permission condition) · IndexTTS-2 (no license tag — treat as blocked). **The legal fit, sharpened (this supersedes §2026-08-06's one-liner "a rented address with real mail deliverability satisfies it" — deliverability alone is NOT enough):**

- **§ 5 ECG wants the *tatsächliche Niederlassung*, not any deliverable address.** WKO's guide: the Anschrift is that of the actual establishment, service-capable for behördliche/gerichtliche Schriftstücke. Two prongs must hold for a rented address: (a) it is genuinely the establishment — in practice the registered Gewerbestandort with contractual usage rights; (b) it is zustellfähig — the provider holds a postalische Empfangsvollmacht covering official mail. A bare Postfach fails both; a pure letterbox that is not the real establishment fails (a) even if mail arrives. (Medium-high confidence — no Austrian court decision directly on point.) Once a Gewerbe exists, § 63 GewO independently forces Name + Standort der Gewerbeberechtigung onto the site — the rented Standort would appear anyway.
- **§ 25 MedienG (kleine-Website floor) never exposed the street:** it asks "Wohnort oder Sitz" as the *municipality only*. Pre-founding, a natural person's disclosed Ort is the REAL residence municipality — a rented address cannot substitute for it (medium confidence: statute wording + WKO's definitional gloss, no case law found). Once an e.U. has its Standort at the rented address, "Sitz: <city>" suffices and the Wohnort drops off. - **Gewerbestandort at a virtual office — yes, with substance:** WKO Wien's founder page says flatly "Postfach oder 'virtuelles' Büro sind als Gewerbeadresse nicht erlaubt"; practice and doctrine reconcile this as *bare letterbox no — a Bürodienstleister's real staffed premises yes*, given a written Nutzungsvereinbarung/Standortbestätigung (the actual Verfügungsrecht), real on-site mail processing, and the § 66 GewO Firmenschild.
