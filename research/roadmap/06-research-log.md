## Case study: Old Greg's Tavern (oldgregstavern.com)

AI dungeon-master platform, solo or up to 5 players, mobile-first (iOS/Android), claims **361,500+ adventurers**. The closest live analogue to our public-launch shape.

- Pricing: **Adventurer $5 one-time → 5 credits + 50 rounds · Hero $15/mo → 20 credits + 200 rounds · Legend $25/mo → 45 credits + 450 rounds** ⇒ **5.6–10 ¢ per round**. Moved from a flat $5 one-time price to tiers in August 2025. First hour free, no credit card.
- A **round** = one full cycle: whole party acts, checks roll, the AI DM responds — bundling several messages into one billable unit.
- **Host pays, guests ride free** — five players, one payer; viral and simple.
- Sources: [pricing page](https://www.oldgregstavern.com/pricing), [landing](https://www.oldgregstavern.com/), [LoreKeeper comparison](https://lore-keeper.com/blog/lorekeeper-vs-old-gregs-tavern), [Fables' own comparison](https://fables.gg/blog/old-gregs-tavern-vs-friends--fables-plan--feature-comparison), [Arcanum review](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/old-gregs-tavern-review/)

## Case study: Friends & Fables (fables.gg)

AI GM ("Franz") + world-building platform. The subscription-shaped alternative.

- Tiers: **Free $0 (25 turns/day cap, 3-player) · Starter $19.95 · Pro $29.95 · Legend $39.95**, paid tiers with "Unlimited Standard Turns", 4/5/6-player campaigns, and **100/300/600 monthly credits**. Prices raised from $14.95–34.95 in Dec 2025. A turn = Franz sends a message. Friends play free with any paid subscriber.
- "Unlimited" survives via: hard-capped free tier, cheap default routing, and **credits gating the expensive paths** (premium narration models, image studio).
- Model mix: **Gemini, Llama, GPT, Grok, custom fine-tunes ("ACE-1")** — many providers behind one internal credit currency, proving the credit is pure bookkeeping.
- Sources: [pricing](https://fables.gg/pricing), [worlds](https://fables.gg/worlds), [plan comparison](https://fables.gg/blog/ai-game-master-vs-friends--fables-plan--feature-comparison), [Dungeons Deep review (models)](https://dungeonsdeep.ai/blog/friends-and-fables-review-2026)

## Verified technical facts (this repo & stack)

- **pi**: MIT (© 2025 Mario Zechner); install via npm `@earendil-works/pi-coding-agent`, standalone **Bun-compiled binaries** in GitHub releases (checksummed source archives + rebuild instructions since v0.81.1); multi-provider LLM API; **no built-in permission system** — the container is the boundary. Re-checked 2026-08-04: **v0.83.0 current** (the version this repo's audit ran against); binaries actively maintained; v0.83 adds **credential-export commands + headless provider sign-in** — directly useful for stage 1 container auth; OAuth logins broadened (OpenRouter, Kimi). [Repo](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi) · [Releases](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi/releases)
- **This repo**: ~3.5 MB game (TS + markdown) + 26 MB yt-dlp submodule; at research time no LICENSE file existed (= all rights reserved by default) — the explicit all-rights `LICENSE` landed 2026-08-04 (R3); `.gitignore` already excludes `auth.json`, `data/`, `tools/ffmpeg/`, session drops.
- **Video path**: `extension/mediasearch.ts` runs `python3 -m yt_dlp` with `PYTHONPATH` at the vendored source → python3 is a runtime dependency of `find_video` only; official yt-dlp standalone binaries exist as a Python-free alternative (code change required).
- **yt-dlp**: Unlicense (public domain) — redistribution unrestricted.
- **Terminal-to-web**: ttyd (xterm.js + libwebsockets; process per connection) is the established bridge; xterm.js is the underlying browser terminal. [ttyd](https://tsl0922.github.io/ttyd/) · [xterm.js](https://xtermjs.org/)
- **Node "protection" reality**: obfuscation raises reading cost only; bytenode (V8 bytecode) is stronger but breaks nothing determined; **Node SEA embeds source readable** — packaging, not protection. None of it can protect markdown. [obfuscator](https://github.com/javascript-obfuscator/javascript-obfuscator) · [bytenode](https://github.com/bawerd/bytenode) · [packaging overview](https://json-server.dev/packaging-nodejs-app-secure-code/)
- **Legal defaults**: no license ⇒ full copyright, viewing ≠ rights ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/articles/licensing-a-repository), [choosealicense](https://choosealicense.com/no-permission/)); PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 = lawyer-written "source visible, no commercial use" ([text](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/noncommercial/1.0.0)).
- **Private distribution channels**: itch.io draft/restricted modes with passwords + download keys ([docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/access-control)); Steam Playtest with controlled invites ([docs](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)) — Steam requires app registration ($100), later-stage only.
- **Normal game anatomy** (for the record): compiled executable + engine runtime + cooked/packed assets (e.g. Unreal `.pak`), source never shipped, decompilation yields approximations — and script-heavy engines (Unity IL, Godot pck, Ren'Py) leak far more. Protection is a gradient ending in law, not walls. ## 2026-08-05 — credential custody: the browser vault (R11 evidence)

Sources verified 2026-08-05; dated posts carry their dates. The maintainer's ruling — durable credentials on the player's machine, server holds them only per-session — matches the healthiest precedent cluster in the BYOK space, and is implementable today with Baseline browser primitives.

- **Storage:** OWASP is flat — no credentials/refresh tokens in localStorage/sessionStorage ("a single XSS discloses every token"; [Session Management](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html), [HTML5](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/HTML5_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html) cheat sheets). The [IETF browser-based-apps BCP](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-browser-based-apps/) (rev 27, 2026-07-06, RFC-editor queue, ~Jan 2027) ranks: BFF > token-mediating backend > browser-held; in-browser: memory > worker > storage. Our shape is deliberately browser-held (the ruling) → so take every hardening rung: **IndexedDB ciphertext under a non-extractable WebCrypto `CryptoKey`** (opaque handle; Apple platforms additionally keychain-wrap it — [dchest.com](https://dchest.com/2025/06/17/how-to-store-web-data-in-keychain/), 2025-06-17), plaintext only inside a dedicated Worker, vault on its **own origin** (game-page XSS can't reach its IndexedDB; postMessage with exact-origin checks), **CSP + Trusted Types** (Baseline since Feb 2026: Chrome 83 '20, Safari 26 '25, Firefox 145 2025-11-11).
- **Key wrap:** preferred **passkey with the WebAuthn PRF extension** → HKDF → AES-256-GCM — Bitwarden's shipped construction ([blog](https://bitwarden.com/blog/prf-webauthn-and-its-role-in-passkeys/), 2025-02-05); 1Password/Dashlane similar. ⚠ PRF support is the fastest-moving fact here (Windows Hello only since the Feb-2026 Win11 update; open WebKit CTAP2 bugs; [Corbado tracker](https://www.corbado.com/blog/passkeys-prf-webauthn) upd. 2026-08-03) — feature-detect, fall back to **passphrase → Argon2id** (WASM; OWASP minimum m=19 MiB/t=2/p=1; PBKDF2-SHA-256 ≥ 600k if WebCrypto-pure — [Password Storage](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html)).
- **Injection:** per-session over TLS to a single-purpose endpoint with **body-logging structurally disabled** (GitHub and Twitter both logged plaintext passwords via incidental paths, May 2018); into **tmpfs only** (Kubernetes Secret-volume semantics: "never written to non-volatile storage"; ⚠ tmpfs can swap — kernel ≥ 6.3 `noswap`, else encrypt swap); never in image layers/build args (`docker history` leaks ARG/ENV — BuildKit `--mount=type=secret`), never env vars (`docker inspect`, `/proc/*/environ`). Wipe = **container teardown**, plus host `LimitCORE=0` (HashiCorp Vault hardening; Böck's coredumps-in-webroots findings). Named ephemerality precedents: Google Cloud Shell (per-user VM, discarded on idle; Ephemeral mode deletes all), GitHub Codespaces (client-side sealed-box secrets, injected at create/resume).
- **The pi-specific wrinkle:** pi **rewrites `auth.json` on OAuth token refresh** — client custody without a rotation **sync-back** channel strands OAuth entries (fine for static API keys). File-watch `auth.json` in-container → re-encrypt to vault at rotation and session end.
- **Precedent record:** client-custody apps run clean (TypingMind — keys "never sent to TypingMind's servers", optional passphrase wrap; big-AGI similar); the documented incidents are server-side stores: SillyTavern key-exfiltration PSA ([#5592](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern/discussions/5592), 2026-05-03) + CVE-2025-59159 (⚠ severity via secondary source — confirm vs NVD before quoting), Chatbot UI hosted-key leak (issue #962, 2023-09-05), LibreChat's server-side AES-CBC store drawing scrutiny (#6678). Counterweight kept honest: custody moves risk to the endpoint, it doesn't abolish it.
- **The honest limit (players get told this):** custody protects against *our* credential store being breached (there is none) and shrinks liability/subpoena scope; it cannot protect against compromise **during an active session** or a malicious server — we ship the JavaScript (Ptacek 2011's standing objection; restated 2024: "You MUST trust your server"). The ecosystem fix to watch is **WAICT** (manifest-pinned app code in transparency logs — [Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/improving-the-trustworthiness-of-javascript-on-the-web/) 2025-10-16, Firefox Nightly prototype May 2026; ⚠ re-check quarterly). Until then the mitigation that works regardless: **scoped keys with provider-side spend limits** — the exact advice SillyTavern's PSA gave its users.

## 2026-08-05 — session shipping & playtest-data consent (R13 evidence)

Sources verified 2026-08-05 unless dated. Context that shaped the design: our "client" is the player's server-side container (durable local append already happens — pi's JSONL), so consumer-SDK machinery for flaky links mostly drops out; container death replaces "app killed" as the failure mode.

**The batching landscape — nobody ships per-event over the network:**

- Unity Analytics: in-memory buffer → **batch upload every 60 s** (non-configurable), up-to-5 MB disk cache on offline shutdown, uploaded next session ([SDK behaviour](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/analytics/sdks-and-apis/sdk-behaviour)). OpenTelemetry BatchSpanProcessor defaults: 5 s delay / 2048 queue / 512 batch ([spec](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/otel/trace/sdk/)). GA4/GameAnalytics guidance: batch, and flush pending events at session end.
- Crash tooling is the crash answer: **Crashlytics stores locally, uploads silently on next launch** ([troubleshooting](https://firebase.google.com/docs/crashlytics/troubleshooting)); Sentry writes envelopes to disk first, retries on connectivity ([offline caching](https://develop.sentry.dev/sdk/foundations/transport/offline-caching/)).
- Server-side games just accumulate server-side: DCSS webtiles runs the Crawl process per player on the server and records ttyrecs/morgues into per-user dirs ([architecture](https://crawl.develz.org/wordpress/webtiles-architecture)) — their community norm makes them *publicly downloadable*; ours stay private (R13 rejection).
- Unreal Insights is the one live-streamer — binary trace packets, LZ4, to a purpose-built local daemon — the exception that proves the boundary rule (purpose-built transport, not the open network).
- Integrity/idempotency: client-chosen idempotency key + retry-safe apply ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/idempotency)); per-file SHA-256 in a manifest, verified store-side (S3 additional checksums); **don't** use multipart ETags as content identity (part-size-dependent). rclone default skip = size+modtime; `--checksum` for the strict variant ([docs](https://rclone.org/docs/)). Compression: **zstd** dominates zlib on every axis for text (2.9× ratio at 510 MB/s vs 2.7× at 105 — [zstd README](https://github.com/facebook/zstd), v1.5.7 Silesia); `tar.zst` for the sealed archive.

**Consent & GDPR practicals (an EU hobby beta is still a controller):**

- The household exemption does not cover operating a hosted service that records other people's sessions (hobby *motive* ≠ household *activity* — [ICO exemptions guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/exemptions/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-exemptions/)); chat/gameplay transcripts keyed to a player are personal data, pseudonymised is still personal ([Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/)); a "don't type personal data" notice is good minimization and **changes nothing about obligations**.
- Cleanest lawful basis for full-transcript recording: **consent at invite-acceptance** (legitimate-interest balancing is a stretch for keystroke-complete transcripts, and consent-then-fallback-to-LI is blocked once you ask). Disclosure minimum ([Art. 13](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/)): who (name + email), what (full transcripts + world files + timestamps + player id), why + basis + withdrawal, **that a human reads sessions**, recipients (host, LLM API provider + link to its data terms ⚠ fast-moving), transfers if non-EU processing (⚠ DPF status volatile — re-verify at public launch), retention criterion, rights incl. complaint; answered within one month ([Art. 12(3)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-12-gdpr/)).
- The cautionary precedent, kept close: **AI Dungeon, April 2021** — users discovered moderators could read private stories; community revolt; policies reversed within months; today they lead with "unpublished single-player content is never moderated" ([slashdot 2021-05-01](https://games.slashdot.org/story/21/05/01/2123201/ai-generated-text-adventure-community-angry-content-moderators-may-read-their-erotica), [current policy](https://help.aidungeon.com/faq/how-does-content-moderation-work) ⚠). Our inversion: the human reader is the *first thing disclosed*.
- Wording models in the wild: ChatGPT's signed-out footer ("Don't share sensitive info. Chats may be reviewed…" — deployed under the [OPC-Canada remedies](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-news/news-and-announcements/2026/bg-info_openai_260506/), 2026-05-06); Unreal's crash-reporter consent checkbox (logs "contain some personal information"). ⚠ Steam-stage note: **Steam Playtests carry no NDA and no secrecy expectation** per Valve ([docs](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)) — NDA-gated testing needs an off-Steam channel.
