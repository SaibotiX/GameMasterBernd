## 2026-08-18 — the hub round: best practices, the allowed-question, alternatives, ground truth

### Is co-hosting several projects on the one VPS allowed?

**Yes — netcup's AGB put no limit on the number of websites, domains or applications on a VPS.** Read 2026-08-18 (netcup.com/de/agb): the binding clauses are content- and abuse-shaped — no bulk mail, no unauthorized copyrighted material, no crypto-mining, no extremist/illegal content — plus a heavy-load clause that **explicitly exempts dedicated and virtual servers** ("Betreiben von Serverdiensten, die eine besonders starke Rechnerlast verursachen; dies gilt nicht bei dedizierten und virtuellen Servern"). Several small personal projects are squarely normal use. ⚠ Re-verify at the hourly→12-month conversion (contract terms are re-accepted at term changes) — trigger already standing in the roadmap.

Domain side: subdomains under worldconsole.eu are ours to create at INWX at any depth, free; no .eu-specific restriction on subdomain use. Let's Encrypt issues per-name at any label depth over HTTP-01 (rate limits ~50 certs/week per registered domain — two orders of magnitude over need).

### Best practices found for a multi-project "hub" (community consensus, 2026-08-18)

- **Hub at the root, one subdomain per app** is the recurring recommended shape for organizing side projects under one domain — a single landing "repository" site linking each project on its own subdomain ([dev.to: Organize Your Side Projects with a Single Domain](https://dev.to/joshhadik/true-your-side-projects-with-a-single-domain-3716); [curious.page: Best Personal Website Examples for Developers](https://curious.page/blog/best-personal-website-examples-developers) — "simplicity wins; the most successful sites are clean, fast, focused").
- **Subdomains over subdirectories for *separate apps*:** cookies/storage scope per origin (path-sharing risks session bleed and forces every app subpath-clean), headers/TLS policy rule per host, and the big precedents run subdomain-per-app (mail./drive./docs.) ([dev.to: subdomains vs virtual hosts](https://dev.to/christianpaez/subdomains-vs-virtual-hosts-2e71); [dev.to poll: subdomains or subdirectories](https://dev.to/madza/do-you-prefer-subdomains-or-subdirectories-4k25)). Subdirectories win only when the parts are one product — not our case.
- **Caddy multi-site: one Caddyfile importing per-site files** — the community's "sites-enabled" layout: snippets/global first, then `import` of a sites directory, one file per site; supports globs and parameterized snippets ([Caddy docs: Caddyfile concepts](https://caddyserver.com/docs/caddyfile/concepts); [caddy.community: organizing sites into multiple Caddyfiles](https://caddy.community/t/organizing-sites-into-multiple-caddyfiles/5921); [sahensley: splitting a Caddyfile](https://sahensley.com/posts/splitting-a-caddyfile-into-multiple-configs/); [timwilson.info: hosting multiple sites with one Caddy proxy](https://timwilson.info/posts/2023/02/hosting-multiple-sites-on-one-host-with-a-caddy-proxy-server/)). The deployment already runs this pattern one level down (`friends/*.caddy`) — the hub is the same move at site level.
- **Wildcard certificates need DNS-01** and, on Caddy, a custom build with a DNS provider plugin; the netcup plugin exists ([caddy-dns/netcup](https://github.com/caddy-dns/netcup)) but needs API credentials and documented ≥900 s propagation waits ([caddy.community thread](https://caddy.community/t/dns-01-challenge-failing-with-netcup-dns-plugin/16652)) — and our DNS is at INWX anyway. Explicit per-name HTTP-01 chosen; zero new machinery.

### The PaaS alternative, priced and declined

Coolify and Dokploy are the self-hosted "hub in a box" products (git-push deploys, dashboard, auto-TLS via their own Traefik). On a 4 GB box the platform overhead is real: **Coolify ~0.5–1.2 GB RAM idle** (5–7 % CPU idle, 25 % spikes), **Dokploy ~350 MB** ([INTROSERV comparison](https://introserv.com/blog/dokploy-vs-coolify-complete-comparison-of-the-best-self-hosted-paas-platforms-for-vps-and-dedicated-servers-2026/); [Contabo blog](https://contabo.com/blog/blog-coolify-vs-dokploy-comparison/); [srvrlss: the true cost of self-hosting](https://www.srvrlss.io/blog/coolify-v-dokploy-v-digitalocean/)). The consensus caveat matches our case exactly: with a working compose + reverse-proxy lane, a PaaS "adds overhead without adding value" ([bitdoze: Coolify vs Dokploy vs Kamal 2](https://www.bitdoze.com/coolify-vs-dokploy-vs-kamal-2/)). Declined in the architecture; revisit trigger named there (tenant count making hand-compose a chore).
